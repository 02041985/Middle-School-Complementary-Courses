v: cross-rhythm :: A rhythm that cuts across the main beat.
v: layer :: One part in a stack of rhythms.

tx: Building 3 against 2 (written for this lesson): ¶ 1. Make a grid of 6 equal pulses: 1 2 3 4 5 6. ¶ 2. Part A (the "3"): play on 1, 3 and 5. ¶ 3. Part B (the "2"): play on 1 and 4. ¶ 4. Together: both hands on 1, right on 3, left on 4, right on 5. A popular memory phrase is "hot cup of tea": "hot" (both), "cup" (right), "of" (left), "tea" (right). ¶ 5. Practise slowly: one hand at a time, then together on a desk, then on drums or body percussion. ¶ 6. Hear it: 3 against 2 is common in West African and Afro-Cuban music; 6/8 bars that switch to feel like 3/4 create hemiola. ¶ 7. Create (20 minutes): In groups of four, build a 12-pulse grid. tx: Recording session (written for this lesson): ¶ 1. Set up (5 minutes): quiet space, headphones on (so the backing track isn't picked up by the microphone), microphone about a hand's width to 20 cm away. ¶ 2. Check levels: sing the loudest part; the meter should peak in the upper-middle, never red (clipping). ¶ 3. Record (15 minutes): record vocals or a live instrument over your arrangement. Record several takes and keep the best, or combine the best parts. ¶ 4. Mix (15 minutes): Levels: vocals or melody loudest, drums and bass steady, chords supporting. Panning: keep vocals, kick and bass centred; pan other parts slightly left or right. Effects: a little reverb gives space; too much sounds muddy. Listen on headphones and speakers. ¶ 5. Export (5 minutes): MP3 for sharing or WAV for best quality. Name: title_yourname. ¶ 6. Share in class and write a reflection: what you are proud of, one thing you'd change and what you learned about songwriting. ¶ Privacy: share only in the class space; ask permission before sharing anyone else's voice.
