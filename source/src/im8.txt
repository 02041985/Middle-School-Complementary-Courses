ts: In Tune Together || Performance || How do I tune my instrument?
tx: Tuning guide (written for this lesson): ¶ 1. Warm up first; cold instruments play flat. ¶ 2. Tuning note: usually Concert B♭ (or Concert A for some groups). ¶ 3. Tuner: play a steady mf note; read the needle. Within about ±5 cents is close. ¶ 4. Adjust: If sharp (too high), lengthen the instrument: pull out the tuning slide, headjoint, barrel or mouthpiece. If flat (too low), shorten it: push in. Make small changes. ¶ 5. By ear: play with a partner or a drone; listen for beats. Faster beats = further apart; beats slow down as you get closer; no beats = in tune. ¶ 6. Playing habits also affect pitch: loud playing can go sharp on some instruments; soft playing can go flat; keep air supported. ¶ Mallet percussion and piano are fixed pitch, so percussionists help by playing the reference pitch and listening.

ts: One Sound || Performance || How does a section blend into one sound?
tx: Blend activities (written for this lesson): ¶ Balance vs blend: balance is about volume between parts; blend is about tone matching so voices merge. ¶ 1. Unison test: a section plays Concert F together. Can you pick out individual players? If yes, adjust. ¶ 2. Match the lead: the lead player plays a note; the next player matches tone, volume and length; then the next joins, until the whole section plays. ¶ 3. Attacks and releases: match how you start ("too" vs "doo") and when you stop. ¶ 5. Vibrato: in a young band, usually play without vibrato in unisons unless the lead player uses it; mismatched vibrato stops blend. ¶ 6. Cross-section blend: clarinets and flutes play the melody together; adjust until they sound like one new colour.

=== IM8.D1 2
hook: Run out of air in the middle of a phrase and the music breaks apart. Good players plan where to breathe, just like singers and swimmers, so every phrase stays whole.
sc: I can choose good places to breathe in my music | I can mark breath points with a breath mark | I can take quick, full breaths without breaking the beat
v: breath mark :: A comma-shaped symbol (') showing where to breathe.
v: phrase ending :: The natural place to breathe at the end of a phrase.
v: catch breath :: A quick breath taken in a short gap.
v: staggered breathing :: Section members breathing at different times to keep a long note going.
v: time-stealing :: Shortening the note before a breath slightly to make time to breathe.
v: breath plan :: A marked map of where to breathe in a piece.
ts: Plan Your Breaths || Performance || Where should I breathe in my music?
tx: Breath-planning guide (written for this lesson): ¶ Good places to breathe: at rests; at the end of phrases (look for long notes and cadences); before a new section or a repeat. ¶ Places to avoid: in the middle of a slur; between a tied note; in the middle of a phrase's climb to the high point. ¶ Steps: 1. Sing or hum your part and breathe where it feels natural. 2. Mark breath marks (') in pencil. 3. If there's no rest, time-steal: shorten the note before the breath slightly. 4. Take quick, low breaths through the mouth; practise breathing in for one beat. 5. Long notes beyond your air: use staggered breathing in sections: each player sneaks out, breathes and slides back in quietly at different times, never at bar lines everyone notices. ¶ Practice: mark a breath plan for the concert ballad; play it through; adjust any marks where you still ran out of air. Compare plans with your section so breaths line up in unison passages.
