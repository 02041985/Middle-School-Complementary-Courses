hook: Choreographers rarely finish a piece in one try. They watch, cut, adjust timing and try again. The difference between a good movement piece and a great one is usually refinement.

ts: Polish the Piece || Performance || How does feedback help an ensemble refine its work?
tx: Feedback protocol (written for this lesson): ¶ 1. Perform: Each group runs its piece once for another group. ¶ 2. Warm feedback (1 minute): Observers say what was clear and powerful: "The canon section looked like a wave of fear." ¶ 3. Cool feedback (1 minute): Observers say what was unclear, using questions: "What was happening in the middle section?" "Were the performers meant to be in unison at the end?" ¶ 4. Performers listen without defending. ¶ 5. Refine (20 minutes): Choose three changes. Common refinements: clean up unison so counts match; smooth transitions so there's no "dead" moment; hold stillness longer; make the beginning and ending shapes clearer; cut anything that doesn't support the theme. ¶ 6. Final performance with a clear start (performers in a starting shape, stillness) and a clear ending (a final shape held for three counts).
