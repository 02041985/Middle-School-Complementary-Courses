hook: Most beginners draw heads too big and legs too short. Artists since ancient Greece have used a simple trick: measure the body in "heads." An adult is about seven and a half heads tall, and once you know the landmarks, figures start to look right.

v: head unit :: The height of the head, used to measure the body.
v: landmark :: A key point on the body used to check proportion, such as the elbow or knee.
v: sighting :: Measuring a model by holding a pencil at arm's length.
v: canon :: A set of rules for ideal proportions.
v: mannequin :: A jointed figure model used for drawing.
ts: Measuring the Figure || Studio || How do artists draw the human body in proportion?
tx: Studio steps (written for this lesson): ¶ 1. Proportion guide (adult figure, about 7½ to 8 heads tall): head = 1; chin to nipples ≈ 1 more; navel ≈ 3 heads down; hip joint and crotch ≈ 4 (the middle of the body); knees ≈ 6; feet at 7½ to 8. Elbows line up with the waist; wrists line up with the crotch; the shoulders are about 2 heads wide. Teenagers are often about 7 heads tall. ¶ 2. Proportion chart (15 minutes): Draw a vertical line and divide it into 8 equal head units. Draw a simple figure of ovals and lines that fits the landmarks. ¶ 3. Sighting (25 minutes): A classmate poses standing (fully clothed, simple pose) for 10 minutes at a time. Hold your pencil at arm's length, measure the head with your thumb, then count how many heads tall the model is. Draw the figure lightly, checking landmarks. ¶ Note: in Grade 9 we draw clothed classmates, mannequins and figures from photos.

tx: Portfolio guide (written for this lesson): ¶ 1. Select (15 minutes): From your Grades 7–9 work, choose 6 to 8 pieces. Include: your strongest work, a work that shows growth (an early piece beside a later one), a work showing a skill (perspective, portrait, printmaking), a series or mixed-media piece, and one that shows your personal style. ¶ 2. Order: Decide how to tell your story: by time (showing growth), by theme, or from strongest to most experimental. ¶ 3. Labels: Write a label for each work: Title, Medium, Size (cm), Year. ¶ 4. Statement: Attach your artist statement from last lesson. ¶ 5. Exhibition (one lesson): Display portfolios as a class exhibition. Visitors leave one comment card per portfolio. ¶ 6. Reflection (10 minutes): Write about how you've grown since Grade 7 and what you want to explore in high school art.
